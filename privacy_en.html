Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 25, 2026

This app (the "App") shows weather and your calendar together, and is offered on Android (Google Play) and iPhone (App Store). The developer (the "Developer") respects your privacy and follows applicable data-protection laws. This policy explains what information the App uses, where it is stored and who receives it.

1. At a glance

  • There is no sign-up or login, and the App does not ask for your name or phone number. On Android you sign in with a Google account only if you use the shared-calendar invitation feature (section 3).
  • The Developer runs no servers for the App and does not collect or store your information.
  • Location is used only to show weather and to write your morning notification. On Android, if you allow it, the App also reads your location once, shortly before the notification, even while the App is closed (section 2). The iPhone version uses your location only while you are using the App.
  • The contents of your calendar events are not sent to the Developer or to the advertising service.
  • The Android version shows Google AdMob ads, and an advertising ID and similar information are sent to Google for that purpose (section 7). The iPhone version currently shows no ads.
  • The Android version uses Firebase Analytics (Google) for usage statistics. Location and calendar content are not included (section 8). The iPhone version does not use it at present.

The iPhone version (1.0) differs from the Android version as follows.

  • Location: used only while you are using the App; the App does not read your location while it is closed ("Always" access). The morning notification is written for the last place the App confirmed.
  • Calendar: the App reads and writes your iPhone's device calendar (the same events as the Calendar app). If you add a Google or other account in the iPhone's settings, that account's events also appear through the device calendar.
  • Google sign-in and shared-calendar invitations are not offered in the iPhone version.
  • Advertising and usage statistics (Firebase) are not used in the iPhone version. If they are introduced, this policy will be updated first and you will be told in the App.

2. Information the App uses, and why

  • Location (precise and approximate): used to show weather, air quality, UV and sunrise/sunset information for where you are. You can turn location permission off at any time in your device settings; the App then shows a default region.
  • Background location ("Allow all the time", Android version): only if you turn on "Use my current location" in the settings and choose "Allow all the time" yourself, the App reads your location once, at low power, about 45 minutes before your morning notification goes out, even while the App is closed. That location is used only to put the weather where you are in your morning notification — never for anything else. If you do not allow it, or turn the feature off, the notification is written for the last place the App confirmed, and that place's name is shown in it. You can turn this off at any time in the settings or your device settings.
  • Last confirmed location: for the feature above, the coordinates, place name and time of the last confirmed location are kept on your device only.
  • Calendar information (event titles, times, locations, notes): used to display the events on your device in the App and in your morning notification, and to write changes to your device calendar when you add, edit or delete an event in the App.
  • Google account: in the Android version, Google sign-in is used when you use the shared-calendar invitation feature (section 3). The App does not sign you in for anything else. To tell calendars apart on screen, the App reads the account name shown in your device's calendar list (such as a Google email address) and displays it on your device only.
  • Camera and photos: used only when you attach a photo to an event note. The App handles only photos you take or choose yourself.
  • Notifications: used to create and show notifications on your device when you turn them on.
  • Search text: the place name you type when you search for a saved location.

3. Google Calendar and Google sign-in

  • Device calendar: when you add a Google account in your device settings and turn on calendar sync, the App reads and writes the Google Calendar events that arrive in your device calendar. Syncing with Google's servers is handled by your device (Android or iPhone) and Google Calendar, under Google's privacy policy.
  • Shared-calendar invitations (Android version): only when you create a calendar in the App and invite someone, the App uses Google sign-in and the Google Calendar API. It asks for these permissions: view and change who a calendar is shared with, view your calendar list and switch a shared calendar on in it, and create a calendar the App makes itself and manage events in it. The email address of the person you invite is sent to Google, which sends the invitation. Google handles your sign-in; the App never receives or stores your password.
  • The App does not send your Google Calendar events or invitation details to the Developer or any third party. This information is used only to provide the features you ask for (showing events, saving, editing and deleting them, and inviting) — never for advertising or analytics.
  • The App's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.
  • If you turn off the calendar permission, turn off sync for that Google account on your device, or remove the App's access in your Google account settings, the App can no longer reach that information.

4. Where information is stored, and for how long

  • Your settings (language, units, which weather items to show), saved locations, the last confirmed location, recent weather and air-quality cache, and event-note photos are stored only on your device.
  • They are deleted when you uninstall the App or clear its data. You can also delete saved locations and note photos inside the App.
  • Calendar events themselves are stored in your device calendar, and events synced with a Google account are also stored on Google's servers. That is handled by your device and Google Calendar, not by the App, and Google's privacy policy applies.
  • How long Google keeps information it handles for advertising follows Google's policies (section 7).

5. Information sent to outside services

To fetch weather and related data, the App sends requests to the services below. These requests are made not only while the App is on screen but, if you allow it, also in the background to prepare your morning notification. Requests contain only what is needed, and the Developer does not receive or store their contents.

  • Korea Meteorological Administration API Hub: receives your location (as grid coordinates) to look up the weather.
  • Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com): receives coordinates or your search text for weather outside Korea, for place search, when estimating how much rain has fallen today, and when you open the hour-by-hour air-quality or UV screens.
  • Korea Environment Corporation (AirKorea): receives the name of your area (city/county level) to look up air quality.
  • Google (sign-in, Google Calendar API, Android version): receives the information described in section 3 when you use shared-calendar invitations. Events written to your device calendar may be synced with your Google account.
  • Google AdMob (Android version): receives the information described in section 7 to show ads.
  • Google Firebase Analytics (Android version): receives the information described in section 8 for usage statistics.
  • Your device's location-name service: may be handled by your device provider (for example Google or Apple) to turn coordinates into a place name.

These services may process request data (such as your IP address) under their own privacy policies.

6. Sharing with third parties and processors

The Developer does not collect or keep your personal information, and does not share it with third parties or use processors for it. The outside services in sections 5 and 7 process the information described there under their own policies, and the advertising service (Google) collects an advertising ID and similar information directly. Even if the Developer received a legal request, it holds no user information.

7. Advertising

The Android version shows Google AdMob (Google LLC) ads at the bottom of the screen and on the weather screen. The iPhone version currently shows no ads.

  • For ads, Google may process an advertising ID, IP address, device information (model, OS version, language and so on), app usage information, ad impressions and clicks, and crash diagnostics, and this may be sent to Google's servers including in the United States. The App does not pass your calendar information or your precise location to the advertising service.
  • Google's handling of advertising information follows Google's policies: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • In your device settings (Settings > Google > Ads) you can turn off ad personalization or delete or reset your advertising ID. Ads still appear, but less personalized.
  • The App is not directed at children and is not set as child-directed for ads.

8. Usage statistics (Firebase Analytics)

The Android version uses Google Firebase Analytics to see statistics such as the number of users, how many come back, and use by app version, in order to improve the App. The iPhone version does not use it at present.

  • Google may process an app-instance ID (a random value that tells installations apart), the advertising ID, device information (model, OS version, language and so on), automatically collected events such as app opens, updates and time in use, and an approximate region (from the IP address).
  • The App does not log any events of its own and does not send location coordinates, calendar events, photos or what you looked up.
  • This information may be sent to Google's servers, including in the United States, and follows Google's policies: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy
  • You can delete or reset your advertising ID in your device settings; uninstalling the App stops the identifier it created from being used.

9. Your rights

  • You can allow or deny location (including background location), calendar, camera and notification permissions at any time in your device settings.
  • You can delete everything the App stores by uninstalling it or clearing its data.
  • You can remove the access you gave the App at any time in your Google account's security settings (third-party apps and services).
  • For any privacy question or request, contact us at the address below.

10. Children

The App is not directed at children under 14 (or under 13 in some regions) and does not knowingly collect their personal information.

11. Security

The App uses encrypted connections (HTTPS) when it contacts outside services, and it does not store your information on the Developer's servers.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new text and effective date will be shown in the App. Changes that are unfavorable to you will be announced before they take effect.
